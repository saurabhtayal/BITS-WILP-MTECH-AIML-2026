Stationarity &
Autocovariance

Forecasting assumes the series' behaviour doesn't change over time — a stationary series. Here's stationarity, autocovariance, white noise vs random walk, and differencing.

What you'll learn

Weak stationarity, autocovariance γ(h) & autocorrelation ρ(h), white noise, random walk, and differencing.

How to use it

Compare a stationary series with a wandering random walk.

The one idea

If the rules stay the same over time, you can forecast.

01 · Stationarity

What stationary means

Weak stationarity: constant mean, constant variance, and autocovariance that depends only on the lag — not on absolute time.

⚡ Stationary vs random walk

Watch white noise stay put while a random walk wanders and its spread grows.

Fair die vs drifting die. Stationary = same odds every throw; non-stationary = the bias drifts, so yesterday's lesson no longer applies.

Bridge → Measuring the dependence.

02 · Autocovariance

γ(h) and ρ(h)

γ(h) = Cov(xₜ, xₜ₊ₕ), with γ(0) = variance. ρ(h) = γ(h)/γ(0), ρ(0)=1, in [−1,1]. For a stationary series these depend only on h.

Bridge → Two canonical series.

03 · Two series

White noise & random walk

White noise

Zero mean, constant variance, no autocorrelation — the ideal residual a good model leaves behind. Stationary.

Random walk

xₜ = xₜ₋₁ + εₜ; variance grows with time, wanders from any fixed mean. Non-stationary.

Differencing. Replace xₜ by xₜ − xₜ₋₁ to remove a trend (a random walk differenced once becomes white noise). Seasonal differencing xₜ − xₜ₋ₛ removes seasonality.

Practice · Exam-style Q&A

Practice questions

Exam-style questions on stationarity and autocovariance.

Q1
State the conditions for (weak) stationarity.Session 13 · conceptual
Constant mean, constant variance, and an autocovariance that depends only on the lag h (not on absolute time).
Q2
Define autocovariance γ(h) and autocorrelation ρ(h).Session 13 · conceptual
γ(h) = Cov(xₜ, xₜ₊ₕ) (γ(0) is the variance); ρ(h) = γ(h)/γ(0), with ρ(0)=1 and −1 ≤ ρ(h) ≤ 1.
Q3
What is white noise, and is it stationary?Session 13 · conceptual
Zero mean, constant variance, and no autocorrelation (γ(h)=0 for h≠0). It is stationary — the ideal residual a good model leaves behind.
Q4
Why is a random walk xₜ = xₜ₋₁ + εₜ non-stationary?Session 13 · conceptual
Its variance grows with time and it wanders without returning to a fixed mean, so the statistical behaviour changes over time.
Q5
How does differencing help, and what does it do to a random walk?Session 13 · numeric
Differencing replaces xₜ with xₜ − xₜ₋₁, removing a linear trend. A random walk differenced once becomes white noise (stationary).

Key takeaways

Constant mean/variance; γ depends only on lag.

2 · γ(h), ρ(h)

Autocovariance and autocorrelation; ρ(0)=1.

3 · Fixes

White noise vs random walk; difference to make stationary.

The thread. Stationary series have constant mean and variance and an autocovariance that depends only on the lag — the condition that makes forecasting possible. White noise is the stationary ideal; a random walk is non-stationary with growing variance; differencing removes trends to restore stationarity.
